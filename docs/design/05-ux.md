# 05 — User experience

The UX has to feel **faster than a whiteboard** while producing **structured, evaluable** input. The biggest risk is that it feels like form-filling. The design answers that with three ideas:

- one canvas, never several;
- flows recorded by clicking through the topology;
- feedback that points at the canvas.

- **Rail and panels.** An icon rail on the left opens Brief, Estimate, Components and Decisions; choosing the open one collapses its panel. The right panel holds Flow and Inspector and collapses to a button. Both give the canvas room for large designs.
- **Scenario steps.** The header lists scenarios as steps with a status dot (green excellent, blue strong, amber passing, red not passing, grey not evaluated). The current one shows its title; the others show it on hover and to screen readers.
- **Results drawer.** Three sizes: its header only (with the score and band), a third of the screen, or most of it for timelines. Opening a view from a collapsed drawer opens it. The score sits in a ring next to dimension bars coloured by score.
- **Palette.** Components are grouped by category (clients, traffic and edge, compute, caching, databases, messaging and streams, coordination, external services), searchable by name, description or capability, and each entry says what it is.
- **Canvas.** Each node shows its kind's icon on a chip in its category colour, its flavor and size, replicas, the entities it stores, faults, and utilisation. The operation being edited draws its edges and step numbers in the accent colour; the selection is ringed; errors are red. Loading or resetting a design fits it to the view.
- **Scenario focus.** Every scenario shares one design, so the canvas fades what only operations outside the scenario's focus use: while S2 asks about redirects, the create path's key service and the worker that scans new links step back. The operation being edited stays in view, in focus or not, and so does whatever is selected or has an error, an open finding or an overload. Parts no operation uses yet, such as a component just added, are not faded. Operation lists put the focus operations first. A button on the canvas turns the fading off, and the choice is remembered.
- **Themes.** Light and dark, following the system until the person picks one (the header toggle cycles system, light and dark; the choice is remembered). Colours are semantic tokens in `index.css` (surfaces, text, lines, accent, four statuses, eight categories); components never use raw palette shades, and charts and edges read the same tokens. A small script sets the theme before first paint. Secondary text meets WCAG AA (4.5:1) on every surface in both themes.

## Resizing and the live canvas (as built, M19)

- **Resizable panels.** The left and right panels and the results drawer resize by dragging their edges. They also resize with the arrow keys on a focused edge (Shift for bigger steps), and a double click resets them. Sizes are remembered across visits. Dragging the drawer below its minimum folds it to its header, and dragging up opens it again.
- **Health on the canvas.** Under the simulated load, a component turns amber when any part of it is above 85% busy, counting its hottest shard. It turns red, with a gentle pulse, when it is overloaded or an injected fault hits it. The pulse stops when the system asks for reduced motion.
- **Findings on the canvas.** A component that an open finding names (directly, or through a flow step that calls it) carries a badge. The badge shows the number of such findings, coloured by the most serious one. Clicking it opens Findings and pins that finding's evidence on the canvas.
- **Moving traffic.** Dots travel along connections that carry traffic in the shown environment or moment.
  - More dots move faster as traffic grows; queue connections move slower.
  - Dots take the colour of the component they reach: red when it fails, amber when it is close to capacity.
  - A button on the canvas turns the animation off, and the choice is remembered. Reduced motion hides it.
- **Playing a timeline.** The Timeline tab's play button moves the moment forward step by step, in about nine seconds for a whole timeline. The canvas shows each moment's load, health and traffic as it plays.

## Onboarding and simpler flows (as built, M20)

- **Welcome.** A first visit opens a short welcome: read the scenario; design it, then say what happens in a flow; get evaluated as you go. It offers three ways in: the guide, an example design, or exploring alone. The help menu reopens it.
- **Getting-started guide.** A checklist in the corner of the canvas ticks itself off from the design:
  1. a service;
  2. a route from the client to it;
  3. the flow for the scenario's operation;
  4. its first step;
  5. every branch answered;
  6. a score.

  It shows the current step, and expands to all six. **Show me** opens the right panel and rings the control to use (the Service entry, the client's handle, Start the flow, the next open branch, the score), with a one-line instruction. New components are placed clear of the card.
- **Example designs.** The help menu lists each problem's reference designs (the corpus goldens) with the scenarios they answer. Opening one replaces the design until undo.
- **Building flows (as rebuilt after M20).** A flow reads as a list of steps from top to bottom, each a sentence ("Look up Link in Redis", "Read Link from PostgreSQL", "Check expiry"). It is a view of the flow graph, and every step and link stays visible; a test checks this on every reference design.
  - **Choosing a step.** One searchable picker lists likely next steps first, then everything the service can do: calls on each component it is connected to (in sentences), decisions, and responses. Choosing adds the step at once. While the picker is open, the components the service can call light up on the canvas; clicking one narrows the list to it.
  - **Patterns.** Under the likely next steps, the picker offers the usual patterns that fit the place, and so does the studio's menu of every lifeline. Choosing one adds its steps as one change, connecting the service to what they call, and they can then be edited one by one.
    - **Cache-aside**, on read paths: look the entity up in a cache, and on a miss read it from the store that holds it and save it in the cache. Shared caches come before caches inside the service.
    - **Insert with a retry**, on write paths of a design that makes keys (an id generator, a random code, a hash): insert if absent, and on a conflict make a new key and try once more; a second conflict answers 503. Behind a step that already made the key, only the second one is made.
    - **Publish through an outbox**, right where a write to a store with transactions has committed: the event that names what the operation does, to the queue that already carries it.
    - In front of a step, every way out of the pattern leads into that step. At an open place, ways out of one step stay open for the next step, and ways out of several steps share the success response.
  - **Adding on the canvas.** "Add steps on the canvas" (the canvas's top right) puts a cursor at the flow's first open place: a branch that leads nowhere yet, or in front of the main path's response. A bar at the bottom says where the next step goes, moves it to the previous or next place, and ends with Done.
    - Clicking a highlighted component (or Enter on it) opens its steps next to it. Choosing one adds it at the cursor, and the cursor moves behind the new step, so a path can be built click by click.
    - Escape closes the menu first, then stops adding. A picker open in the Flow panel closes once a step is added on the canvas.
    - Each step's number on a connection is a button: it opens the Flow panel at that step and flashes it.
    - While steps are added on the canvas, the Flow panel's main list keeps its picker closed, so one place says where the next step goes.
  - **The command line ("/").** The bar has a line to type the step instead. "/" anywhere outside a text field starts adding on the canvas (at the place a picker in the Flow panel had, else the first open place) and focuses it; without a flow, or with nowhere to add, a toast says why.
    - It offers what the picker offers at that place, in the same order: likely next, patterns, calls on connected components, decisions, responses, then calls that connect the service. A choice matches when it holds every word typed, in any order, including what it is ("pattern", "decide", a component's name).
    - The arrows move through the matches and Enter adds the highlighted one; the line clears and the place moves behind what was added, so a path can be typed step by step. Escape clears what was typed, then stops adding.
  - **The flow studio.** "Open in the studio" (Flow panel) or the canvas's studio button covers the app with the operation's flow as a sequence diagram:
    - **Lifelines:** the source (the client, the queue a consumer reads, or the schedule), the handler, and what it calls in the order it first calls it. "Call another" adds a lifeline to call next.
    - **Rows:** each step is a numbered message to its lifeline, or a note on the handler's for a decision, with where its other outcomes go. A branch is a framed block, a response a return arrow to the source. Each step shows the share of the selected environment's requests that reach it.
    - **Adding:** between rows, a "+" on each lifeline adds what that lifeline offers there: a call on a component, a decision on the handler, a response on the source at an open end. An outcome that leads nowhere offers everything.
    - **Around it:** the operations and how complete each flow is on the left, and the Flow panel on the right, where choosing a step in the diagram brings it up. Escape closes a menu first, then the studio.
  - **What-if traffic.** "Traffic ×1" (the canvas's top right) multiplies the shown environment's users' traffic, from ×0.25 to ×100: every request operation's rate and the clients' connections. Scheduled jobs keep their rate, and consumers follow what is published.
    - The canvas, Load, the pools and the studio show that load, with a banner that says it is a what-if. The score and the findings stay at the scenario's own traffic.
    - "Find the breaking point" looks for the multiple at which the design first breaks, to within 1% (up to ×100). It says what gives first: a component over capacity, a bulkhead, pool or timeout giving up on calls, an operation below 99.9%, or a consumer falling behind. "Show it" jumps there.
  - **Calling what is not connected yet.** Components the service is not connected to are listed too, under "Not connected yet", which opens when a search or a click on the canvas finds one. Choosing one of their steps connects the service to it in the same change. A connection has no mode of its own: a publish that does not wait (fire-and-forget, or through an outbox) is drawn dashed, as is a queue's delivery to its consumer.
  - **Outcomes that leave the list** show under each step as chips: "not found → 404", "error → 503", "hit → steps below". A new step's outcomes get the conventional handling:
    - a cache miss, or a cache that cannot answer, carries on to the next step;
    - a failed cache write does not stop the request;
    - errors answer 503, not found 404, invalid input 400, conflicts 409 and expired data 410;
    - a cache hit answers with the success status the candidate chose.

    A chip changes in place: end with another status, carry on like the main outcome, go to a numbered step, or do something else first (steps of its own, shown indented under "If hit:").
  - **The end of the list** is the response ("Respond 302 Found"), changeable in place, with "Add a step before responding". Between steps, a + inserts a step that carries on into the next.
  - **Fewer chores.** Removing a step joins what came before to what followed. Replacing a branch removes the steps it cut off. Calling a store binds the entity to it when needed. Settings (TTL, batch size, outbox…) show as badges on the step and open from its menu.

## Modes

| Mode | Canvas shows | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Topology only. A coverage matrix lists which operation flows use which components, flags unused components, and summarises the scenario × dimension heatmap. | The whole-design view |
| **Scenario** | Topology plus an overlay of the focus operations' flows. Components outside the focus are dimmed (as built: see Scenario focus). Faulted components get a hatch pattern and an icon. After evaluation, utilisation heat is shown. | Working through S1…Sn |
| **Sandbox** | Free design with no problem. Workload and faults are set by the user. Nothing is scored. | Exploration and learning |
| **Compare** (as built, M17) | Two attempts side by side, run against the same scenario script, with score and cost deltas | Trade-off study |

## Flow recorder

Flows are created by **clicking**, not by drawing arrows:

1. Pick an operation, for example `redirect`. The ingress route is derived and shown (client → CDN → LB → link-api). If several routes exist, a chooser appears.
2. Press **Record**. The handler (`link-api`) is the active actor.
3. Click a target component. Only targets reachable through a connection are clickable. If none exists, the UI offers "create connection" inline.
4. A popover lists the **actions the target offers** (for a cache: `get`, `set`, `setNegative`, `delete`) and asks for the entity and parameters (key, TTL).
5. The step appears in two places:
   - as a numbered badge on the connection on the canvas;
   - as a node in the **step tree** in the side panel.
6. Its outcomes appear as **chips** (`hit`, `miss`, `error`). Choose a chip to continue that branch. Unhandled chips stay amber until resolved.
7. Local logic (`checkExpiry`, `validateUrl`, …) and `respond` (status picker) come from a toolbar in the step tree.

The **step tree is the source of truth**. It supports reorder, re-link of an outcome, delete with automatic re-linking, and copying a branch. The canvas overlay is a projection of it. Both stay in sync, and selection in one highlights the other.

**Visual language.**

| Element | Encoding |
|---|---|
| Outcomes | hit/ok = green, miss/notFound = amber, error = red, conflict = violet. Each also has an icon and a line pattern, so colour is never the only signal. |
| Messaging | dashed: nothing waits on it (publishes without waiting, through an outbox, or a queue's deliveries) |
| Faulted components | hatched |
| Steps | numbered in depth-first order |

**Macros.** Common shapes (cache-aside read, conditional insert with retry) are offered only *after* a scenario using them has passed, or as hint level 3. They save time without giving the answer away.

## Results dock

- **Findings.** Grouped by gate, then severity. Each finding expands to: what we checked, what we found, evidence, why interviewers care, fix, requirement, and learn more. Hovering a finding highlights its evidence on the canvas. Clicking it selects and zooms.
- **Metrics.**
  - Per operation: p50/p95/p99, error rate, availability, and throughput.
  - Per component: load, utilisation (with per-shard detail), hit ratio, and cost.
  - For a database with a volume (M24): the volume's utilisation, marked when it is what limits the store, with its page cache's hit ratio and a copy's I/Os in the tooltip, and the provisioned I/Os' monthly cost. The inspector's Volume section sets the I/Os provisioned on each copy and shows the same at the scenario's environment.
  - Each scenario environment (for example `shard-down`, `cache-cluster-down`) is a tab.
- **Paths.** The **path explorer** lists every enumerated path of the focus operations. For each path it shows the probability, the latency distribution (p50/p99), the terminal status, and the outcome sequence, for example `miss → found → valid → populate → 302`. Selecting a path lights it up on the canvas. This view makes the abstract concrete and is the main teaching surface.
- **Signals.** Senior and staff signals, demonstrated and not yet demonstrated. The ones not yet demonstrated show a one-line nudge, not the answer.
- **Timeline** (as built, M10).
  - Selecting the tab enlarges the dock; a button switches between the two sizes.
  - A timeline with predictions asks them first. The candidate answers, then reveals the charts, or skips straight to them. Answers are kept per timeline in the browser, shown next to what happened ("You said: … It was: …"), and can be predicted again.
  - A scrubber picks a moment. Clicking a chart or an event in the log jumps there, and the arrow keys move a focused chart one step at a time. Each chart marks the moment and its legend shows the values at it.
  - At the picked moment, the canvas shows that moment's load and faults instead of the selected environment, with a banner saying so and a way back.
  - Under the charts, the path mix lists how requests went at that moment: each path's share, status and mean latency. Expanding a path shows each call's latency at the component. A share that never reaches the handler's steps (shed on the way in, or waiting in a backlog) is called out.
- **Score.** The band, the dimension bars, the recognised strategies (for example "Uniqueness: random code + conditional write"), and "approach not recognised" notes.

## Cross-scenario awareness

- Every edit re-evaluates every scenario, debounced to about 300 ms in a Web Worker.
- The scenario rail shows a regression marker when a scenario that previously passed stops passing, and a toast names the change: "Reducing cache TTL to 30 s: S3 hit ratio 0.91 → 0.78 (still passing), S5 shard utilisation 0.74 → 0.88 (now failing)".
- Each scenario keeps a snapshot from when it first passed, so the candidate can diff "what I had then" against "what I have now".

## Learning scaffolding

- **Hints in three levels:** a nudge ("What happens on a miss?"), then a concept ("cache-aside populates after reading the source of truth"), then a specific hint ("add `cache.set` after `store.read=found`, with a TTL"). Using hints is recorded and shown in the debrief, never as a penalty.
- **Debrief** (unlocked when a scenario passes):
  - what interviewers probe next;
  - common mistakes;
  - signals still available;
  - a short "how this works in production" note;
  - links to concept cards.
- **Concept cards** come from the catalog (component explanations, trade-off cards). The content of the original simulator can be adapted here, with attribution.
- **Predict, then reveal** (as built, M10): before a timeline is shown, the candidate can pick what will happen, for example "How busy does the link store get right after the restart? Under half its capacity / Busy but within capacity / Over capacity". The choice is shown next to the result. It is optional, structured, and deterministic: the engine answers each question from the timeline it simulated.

## Estimation gym (as built, M11)

- An **Estimate** tab next to the brief asks the problem's back-of-envelope questions: links a day, storage over five years, peak redirects, the code space, cache memory, clicks a day, click-log bandwidth.
- Answers are typed the way one writes them on a whiteboard (`86M`, `158 billion`, `79 TB`, `3.5 × 10^12`, `60 MB/s`); the field says how it read them.
- Each answer is judged spot on (within 25%), close (within the question's tolerance, 2× by default) or off, with how many times too high or too low. "Show how" gives the answer and its derivation.
- Answers are part of the design: they undo, save and export with it, and do not trigger an evaluation.
- The brief keeps derived numbers (158 B links, 79 TB) back until the estimates are answered, or until the candidate asks for them.

## Step settings (as built, M11)

Call steps have a settings editor for their tunable parameters: TTL, batch size, single-flight, aggregation window, counter shards, fire-and-forget and the buffer size. It offers only what the action takes and the flow can use (no aggregation windows in request handlers, no batches a request would wait for), and applies the changes as one undoable step.

## Compare mode (as built, M17)

The Compare tab runs two designs through every scenario: the current one, and a pinned baseline or a design loaded from a file (the current design stays as it is). For each scenario it shows both scores, the change, and what each would cost a month at its most expensive environment, followed by the list of design changes.

## Productivity details

- **Keyboard:**
  - `R` record flow, `Esc` stop;
  - `1`–`9` choose an outcome chip;
  - `Del` delete, with automatic re-link;
  - `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` / `Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+Z` undo/redo;
  - `Cmd/Ctrl+K` command palette (add component, jump to scenario, open finding).
- **Smart defaults:**
  - new connections get sensible policies (timeout from catalog defaults, no retries);
  - new components get the smallest size that fits the scenario's average load, never the peak load, so the candidate still has to think about peak.
- **Auto-layout** (ELK) for tidying, applied only on request.
- **Persistence:** attempts autosave to IndexedDB with versioned JSON export/import.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- Colour is never the only encoding: icons and patterns accompany it.
- Contrast meets WCAG AA in light and dark themes (as built, M18: body and secondary text are at least 4.5:1 on every surface).
- Every canvas action has a keyboard and panel equivalent, through the step tree and the inspector.
- Desktop (≥ 1280 px) is the primary target. Tablets get a usable layout with collapsible panels. Phones get a read-only review of attempts and results.
